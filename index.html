Part 1 · Diagnosis · The Business

Start With the Marketplace

Juurnee is a two sided marketplace. Its health isn't a page speed score, it's match rate, liquidity and retention. So the diagnosis starts with the business, and the platform waits its turn.

1.1 The questions that come before any platform decision

These are the answers that would actually move my recommendation. None of them are technical.

  • What share of Juurnee requests get a successful match?
  • What's the average time to match, and the spread?
  • What's the supply and demand ratio, by route?
  • Companion retention. Do they take a second and third trip?
  • Seeker retention. What's the repeat booking rate?
  • Referral rate. Are network effects starting?
  • Revenue per user and per successful trip?
  • Where does trust break (KYC drop off, no shows, reviews)?
The line that frames everything Until these are known, any platform decision is secondary. Replatforming a marketplace you can't measure is rebuilding a car while blindfolded to which wheel is flat. Visibility first, always.

1.3 The dynamics that drive the tech

liquidity

Supply is the bottleneck

In most marketplaces the scarce side limits growth. If Companions are thin on key routes, no amount of Seeker acquisition helps. It just lengthens time to match. That makes the matching engine and supply analytics the highest leverage builds.

trust

Trust is the product

People are handing over a child, an elderly parent, or themselves. ID verification isn't a feature, it's the trust layer the whole marketplace rests on. The friction it adds versus the trust it buys is worth measuring, not guessing.

cold start

Density beats reach

One route with reliable liquidity beats fifty routes with none. That argues for concentrating supply geographically before expanding, which is a commercial call the data should drive.

1.4 Assumptions I'm carrying

  • The marketplace, not the stack, is the main risk. A fast platform serving a marketplace we can't measure still fails.
  • Bubble was the right early call. It got Juurnee to 16,000 users. The question is the right next move, not an apology for the last one.
  • Visibility is independent of the platform question, so we do it first, in days, not quarters.
  • There is already a v2. I understand a remake is sitting in Claude Code and GitHub, so week one I assess its readiness rather than assume a clean slate.
  • Continuity is not negotiable. No plan that freezes the product for a rebuild is acceptable at this stage.

Part 2 · Recommendation · The Build

The v2 Decision

A remake already exists in Claude Code and GitHub. That changes the question. It isn't "what stack should we build." It's "is the thing we already have safe to ship, and how do we launch it without breaking 16,000 people."

4.1 The v2 readiness verdict

My first platform job is an audit, not a rebuild. It ends in one of three verdicts, and I'd be honest with you about which one we're in.

VERDICT 1 · SHIP

Ship v2

If the build has clean architecture, working auth, payments, KYC and matching, sound admin flows, real tests and a working deploy path, then the best move is to ship it quickly behind a phased rollout. We don't gold plate a thing that already works.

VERDICT 2 · SHIP WITH GUARDRAILS

Ship v2 carefully (the likely case)

If it mostly works but has gaps, we launch behind feature flags to limited cohorts, with monitoring and instant rollback, and close the gaps in priority order while Bubble keeps serving everyone else. This is where most real codebases sit, and it's my default expectation until the audit says otherwise.

VERDICT 3 · HOLD

Don't ship v2 yet

If it has poor data modelling, insecure auth, broken payment flows, no testability or unclear ownership, then we treat it as a prototype, keep Bubble as the system of record, and rebuild only the risky parts rather than starting again.

4.2 What I'd audit to reach the verdict

A code and architecture review against a fixed checklist, so the verdict is evidence, not vibes.

  • Data model. Is it sound and exportable, or grown organically?
  • Auth and security. How are sessions, secrets and PII handled?
  • Payments. Are flows correct, idempotent and tested?
  • KYC. Does identity verification actually work end to end?
  • Matching and admin. Is the core logic there and operable?
  • Tests and deploy. Is there coverage and a path to production?
  • Ownership. Who built it, is it documented, can we maintain it?
  • Data migration. Can 16,000 users move across cleanly?

4.3 How we ship safely, whatever the verdict

This is the answer to the brief's migration question. The 16,000 users are protected the same way regardless of which path the audit puts us on.

  • Keep Bubble live until v2 has earned its place. No cliff edge.
  • Phased rollout by cohort, behind feature flags, watching the metrics at each step.
  • Instant rollback. If a flow regresses, we flip the flag back. Nothing is one way.
  • Parallel run on the data layer, with a clean canonical store both systems agree on.
  • No feature freeze. We keep shipping to users throughout. The commercial constraint drives the technical plan.

Judgement

Risks, and What Would Change My Mind

A plan with no stated risks is a plan I don't trust. Here is where I think I could be wrong, and the findings that would move me off this recommendation. I'm committed to the goal, which is marketplace growth, not to any one technical path.

6.1 The honest risks in my recommendation

1

The problem might not be technical

Analytics could show people simply don't want the product enough yet. More engineering won't fix demand, so I want to know this early, before we invest in a rebuild.

2

v2 code quality could be poor

The remake might need substantial rework. If the audit finds insecure auth or broken payments, my timeline stretches and the honest move is to say so, not to ship on hope.

3

Liquidity could be the real bottleneck

If we're short on Companions on key routes, code doesn't create supply. The fix becomes a growth and operations problem, and the marketing co-founder leads it, not me.

4

Migration could distract from growth

Any replatforming pulls focus. Without strict sequencing it can quietly stall acquisition and retention, so it has to stay subordinate to the commercial goal.

The point of month oneThe whole purpose of the first month is to shrink these uncertainties before we make any large, expensive or hard to reverse commitment.

6.2 What would change my recommendation

If the evidence comes back differently, so does the plan. That isn't indecision, it's the opposite of being attached to a solution.

Execution · Partnership

Working With the Growth Co-Founder

The marketing co-founder owns growth, acquisition and retention. My job is to make their job possible and measurable, and to turn the two of us into one loop the whole company runs on.

8.1 The weekly exchange

Every week, a two way trade. Neither of us works in a vacuum.

they bring

Marketing to me

  • Acquisition numbers and CAC by channel
  • Campaign results
  • Customer interviews and the customer's actual words
  • Prioritised, evidence backed hypotheses
I bring

Me to marketing

  • Funnel analysis and the biggest leaks
  • Experiment results
  • Product and behavioural insight
  • Retention and cohort analysis

Then together we pick the biggest bottleneck, the biggest opportunity, and the next experiment. That meeting is the company's steering wheel.

8.2 The loop we both serve

Acquisition→ Activation→ Matching→ Trip completion→ Retention→ Referral

Referral feeds back into acquisition. Close the loop and growth compounds. Marketing owns the ends, I own the instrumentation and the matching core in the middle, and we share the metrics across all of it.

8.3 Prioritising together, and what healthy looks like

One backlog, scored openly

A single list scored with RICE. They own reach and impact, I own effort and feasibility. Growth work and tech debt sit in the same list, so there's never a hidden engineering tax.

One cadence, one scoreboard

A weekly sync against one north star metric and its inputs. Trade-offs made visible in the room. When we disagree, the data and the weights break the tie, not seniority.

8.4 One thing I'd ask for, gently

“

Beyond the operating rhythm, there's something I'd genuinely value, and I'd never force it because everyone has a life outside work and I respect that completely. I'd love for the three of us to get out together every so often, maybe once a month, nothing formal.

The best team I've been part of was together for only twelve weeks, and it went extraordinarily well. A lot of that came down to the fact that we actually knew each other. When you're in sync as people, two things get easier. You give each other honest feedback without spending energy worrying about whether it'll land the wrong way. And you stay transparent about where your work really is, because it feels like a shared effort rather than a quiet contest to be the one who looks best.

I've seen first hand how much faster a team moves when that trust is already there, so it's something I'd want to build with you and the wider team from early on.

A.5 Glossary for non-technical reading

Feature flag

A switch that turns a feature on for some users without a new release, so we can roll out gradually and roll back instantly.

Liquidity

Whether there's enough supply (Companions) to serve demand (Seekers) quickly. Low liquidity means long waits, which means churn.

PostHog

A product analytics tool with funnels, retention, session replay and feature flags. It bolts onto the current app today.

RICE

A way to score what to build next, using reach, impact, confidence and effort. It turns prioritising into a shared, honest calculation.
