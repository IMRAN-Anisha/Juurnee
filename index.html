Technical Co-Founder Juurnee · by Anisha Imran

Grow the marketplace.
The platform is how we get there.

Before I rebuild anything, I want to see what is actually driving matches, drop-offs, trust and revenue. Right now we cannot, and that is the real problem. So I would make the marketplace visible first, then make the platform fast enough to act on what we learn.

THE SHORT VERSION

What I would do

Juurnee's value is its marketplace: Seekers who need a Companion, and Companions who get paid to travel. The questions that decide our future are about that, not the stack. How many requests get matched, how fast, and whether people come back. We cannot answer them yet. So week one I turn on the analytics. A v2 already exists, so my first platform job is not to pick a stack, it is to audit that build and decide whether it is safe to ship, ship behind guardrails, or hold. We keep Bubble live until v2 earns its place, protect the 16,000 users with phased rollout and instant rollback, and rebuild only the parts the data and the code review prove we need to.

Part 1 · Diagnosis · Visibility

Funnel Visibility

"Flying blind" is your own phrase, and it is the fastest, highest leverage thing I can fix. It does not wait on any platform decision. There are three layers of visibility, and each one helps a different part of the business.

2.1 Three layers of funnel visibility

Think of it as a funnel with three bands. The widest, top of funnel, is where people come from. The middle is what they do inside the product. The bottom is what turns into money.

Top of funnel · where users come from
Examples

Instagram, Google, PR, referrals, paid ads

How I would track it

UTM links, Google Analytics, PostHog source tracking, and a signup question "where did you hear about us?"

Middle of funnel · what users do inside
Examples

Sign up, verify ID, create a request, browse Companions

How I would track it

PostHog events, product funnels, and session replay to see why people drop, not just where

Bottom of funnel · what becomes value
Examples

Match made, payment, subscription, completed trip, repeat trip

How I would track it

Stripe events, backend events, and clean records in the database or CRM

Why the top layer wins us a marketing partner If we add one question at signup, "where did you hear about us," we get exact numbers on where people actually come from. Say 90 percent arrive from Instagram but only 10 percent of effort goes there. That is a concrete, valuable insight I can hand the marketing co-founder on day one, and it also improves the website by telling us which entry points to polish.

2.2 Is event tracking and PostHog enough to start? Yes, and here is why

The first layer does not need anything clever. We define a clean set of events and watch how many people make it from one step to the next. One concrete example makes it obvious.

The leak, made visible

If 1,000 people arrive, 400 sign up, 180 verify ID, 90 create a Juurnee, 30 get matched and 12 pay, we instantly know where the biggest leak is.

1000arrive› 400sign up› 180verify ID› 90request› 30matched› 12pay

Here the biggest fall is at ID verification. That tells us exactly where to focus, and it is a number anyone on the team can read.

Part 2 · Recommendation · The Build

The v2 Decision

A remake already exists in Claude Code and GitHub. That changes the question. It is not "what stack should we build," it is "is the thing we already have safe to ship, and how do we launch it without breaking 16,000 people."

4.1 The v2 readiness verdict

My first platform job is an audit, not a rebuild. It ends in one of three verdicts, and I would be honest with you about which one we are in.

VERDICT 1 · SHIP

Ship v2

If the build has clean architecture, working auth, payments, KYC and matching, sound admin flows, real tests and a working deploy path, the best move is to ship it quickly behind a phased rollout. We do not gold plate something that already works.

VERDICT 2 · SHIP WITH GUARDRAILS

Ship v2 carefully, the likely case

If it mostly works but has gaps, we launch behind feature flags to limited cohorts, with monitoring and instant rollback, and close the gaps in priority order while Bubble keeps serving everyone else. This is where most real codebases sit.

VERDICT 3 · HOLD

Do not ship v2 yet

If it has poor data modelling, insecure auth, broken payment flows, no testability or unclear ownership, we treat it as a prototype, keep Bubble as the system of record, and rebuild only the risky parts rather than starting again.

4.2 How I would review the v2 build

This is the concrete, hands-on part. A code and architecture review against a fixed checklist, so the verdict is evidence rather than opinion, and so you can see I know what production ready actually means.

  • Can I run it locally from the README?
  • Are environment variables documented and kept separate from secrets?
  • Is auth handled safely, with sessions and tokens done properly?
  • Are protected routes actually protected, server side?
  • Are Stripe webhooks verified against their signature?
  • Are payment actions idempotent so users cannot be charged twice?
  • Is KYC and personal data stored and encrypted safely?
  • Are database permissions and row level access correct?
  • Are there tests for auth, payments, KYC and matching?
  • Is there error logging and monitoring, through Sentry or similar?
  • Is there a staging environment before production?
  • Can we roll back if a launch fails, and is there a clean data path from Bubble?

4.3 How we ship safely, whatever the verdict

This is the answer to the brief's migration question. The 16,000 users are protected the same way regardless of which path the audit puts us on.

  • Keep Bubble live until v2 has earned its place. No cliff edge.
  • Phased rollout by cohort, behind feature flags, watching the metrics at each step.
  • Instant rollback. If a flow regresses, we flip the flag back. Nothing is one way.
  • Parallel run on the data layer, with a clean store both systems agree on.
  • No feature freeze. We keep shipping to users throughout.

Judgement

Risks, and What Would Change My Mind

A plan with no stated risks is a plan I do not trust. Here is where I think I could be wrong, and the findings that would move me off this recommendation. I am committed to the goal, which is marketplace growth, not to any one technical path.

6.1 The honest risks in my recommendation

1

The problem might not be technical

Analytics could show people simply do not want the product enough yet. More engineering will not fix demand, so I want to know this early, before we invest in a rebuild.

2

v2 code quality could be poor

The remake might need substantial rework. If the audit finds insecure auth or broken payments, my timeline stretches, and the honest move is to say so, not to ship on hope.

3

Liquidity could be the real bottleneck

If we are short on Companions on key routes, code does not create supply. The fix becomes a growth and operations problem, and the marketing co-founder leads it, not me.

4

Migration could distract from growth

Any replatforming pulls focus. Without strict sequencing it can quietly stall acquisition and retention, so it has to stay subordinate to the commercial goal.

The point of month oneThe whole purpose of the first month is to shrink these uncertainties before we make any large, expensive or hard to reverse commitment.

6.2 What would change my recommendation

If the evidence comes back differently, so does the plan. The strongest technical co-founder is not the one who defends their first answer, it is the one who updates quickly when the evidence changes.
